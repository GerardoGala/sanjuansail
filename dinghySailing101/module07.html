Module 7

Safety

Good sailors are safe sailors. Learn the essential safety practices that will help you enjoy every sailing session while protecting yourself and others.

Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Identify the essential safety equipment for dinghy sailing.
  • Evaluate whether conditions are suitable for sailing.
  • Recognize common hazards on and around a sailboat.
  • Know what to do in an emergency.
  • Develop safe habits before, during, and after every sail.

Safety Begins Before Launching

Most sailing accidents can be prevented before the boat even enters the water. Always inspect your boat, check the weather forecast, and ensure all required equipment is onboard. Never launch if you are unsure about the conditions or your equipment.

If you are new to sailing, stay close to shore and sail only in light to moderate winds under the supervision of an experienced sailor or instructor.

Essential Safety Equipment

🦺 Personal Flotation Device (PFD)

Always wear a properly fitted life jacket whenever you are on or near the water.

🌤 Weather Forecast

Check the wind strength, weather forecast, tides, and any storm warnings before launching.

📱 Communication

Carry a waterproof means of communication whenever practical and let someone ashore know your sailing plans.

🚤 Safety Boat

Beginners should sail where rescue assistance or a safety boat is available.

Common Hazards

The greatest hazards in dinghy sailing are usually not the boat itself but changing weather, collisions, shallow water, docks, and loss of control during maneuvers. Stay alert, keep a good lookout, and always leave enough room to avoid other boats.

  • Sudden increases in wind strength.
  • The swinging boom during tacks and gybes.
  • Capsizing in strong winds.
  • Fatigue, dehydration, and sun exposure.
  • Shallow water, rocks, reefs, and submerged objects.

If You Capsize

Stay calm. Your dinghy is designed to float even when it is on its side. Check that everyone is safe, remain with the boat, and right the dinghy using the proper capsize recovery technique. Climbing back aboard is usually much easier than attempting to swim to shore.

Good Sailing Habits

  • Always wear your PFD.
  • Check the weather before every sail.
  • Tell someone where you are sailing and when you expect to return.
  • Never sail beyond your skill level.
  • Stay hydrated and protect yourself from the sun.
  • Respect other boats and give way when necessary.
  • When in doubt, return to shore.

Key Takeaways

  • Safety begins before launching.
  • Always wear a properly fitted life jacket.
  • Check the weather and know your limits.
  • Stay with your boat if you capsize.
  • Good judgment is your most important safety equipment.
