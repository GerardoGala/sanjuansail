Module 5

Vang

Learn how the vang controls boom position, leech tension, and sail twist to help manage sail shape and power.

Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Identify the purpose of the vang.
  • Explain how vang tension affects the mainsail's leech.
  • Explain what is meant by sail twist.
  • Recognize the difference between a tightly controlled leech and a twisted sail.
  • Understand how vang tension can help control sail power in different conditions.

What Is the Vang?

The vang is a control system that connects the boom to the lower part of the mast.

Its primary purpose is to control the vertical position of the boom and the tension along the lower leech of the mainsail.

This makes the vang an important sail-shape control, especially when the mainsheet is eased.

Vang and Mainsheet Do Different Jobs

The vang and mainsheet both influence the position and shape of the mainsail, but they do not perform exactly the same function.

Mainsheet

The mainsheet primarily controls the boom's position relative to the centerline of the boat.

Vang

The vang primarily controls the boom's vertical position and helps control leech tension and sail twist.

What Is Sail Twist?

Sail twist is the change in the angle of the sail from the bottom of the sail to the top.

The upper part of the mainsail can be angled differently from the lower part. This is called twist.

Some twist is useful because the wind conditions experienced by the upper and lower parts of the sail are not always the same.

The vang helps the sailor control how much the upper part of the mainsail is allowed to twist.

Tightening the Vang

Tightening the vang pulls the boom downward.

This increases tension along the lower part of the leech and reduces the amount of twist in the sail.

A tighter vang therefore keeps the leech more controlled and can help maintain a consistent sail shape.

Easing the Vang

Easing the vang allows the boom to rise more freely.

This allows the leech to open and the upper part of the sail to twist more.

More twist can allow the upper part of the sail to spill some power, which can be useful in stronger or gusty conditions.

The Vang When Reaching

The vang becomes especially important when sailing on a reach.

As the boat bears away from the wind, the mainsheet is eased and the boom moves farther outboard.

Once the mainsheet is eased, it may no longer provide enough downward force on the boom to control the leech effectively.

The vang can provide that downward force and help maintain the desired sail shape.

Vang and Strong Wind

In stronger wind, the sailor may use vang adjustment to manage the amount of twist and power in the sail.

The correct setting depends on the boat, sail, point of sail, and current conditions.

The goal is not simply to pull the vang as tight as possible. The goal is to achieve an efficient sail shape for the conditions.

The Vang Works With Other Controls

The vang should not be viewed as an isolated control.

It works together with the mainsheet and outhaul.

The mainsheet controls the boom angle. The outhaul controls the tension along the foot. The vang controls boom height and helps control the leech and twist.

Intermediate sailing is about learning how these controls interact rather than treating each control independently.

Practice Exercise

In moderate wind, observe the upper part of the mainsail while adjusting the vang.

  1. Begin with a moderate vang setting.
  2. Tighten the vang gradually and observe the leech of the mainsail.
  3. Ease the vang gradually and observe the increase in twist.
  4. Compare the shape of the upper sail with the lower sail.
  5. Practice making small adjustments while maintaining a balanced boat.

The objective is to learn to recognize how vang tension changes the shape and twist of the mainsail.


Key Takeaways

  • The vang controls the boom's vertical position.
  • Vang tension affects the tension of the mainsail's leech.
  • A tighter vang generally reduces sail twist.
  • An eased vang generally allows more sail twist.
  • The vang becomes especially important when the mainsheet is eased while reaching or sailing downwind.
  • The vang works together with the mainsheet and outhaul to control sail shape and power.
