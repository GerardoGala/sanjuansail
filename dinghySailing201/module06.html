Module 6

Downhaul

Learn how the downhaul controls luff tension and the forward shape of the mainsail.

Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Identify the purpose of the downhaul.
  • Explain how downhaul tension affects the shape of the mainsail.
  • Understand the relationship between downhaul tension and luff tension.
  • Recognize when additional downhaul tension may be useful.
  • Understand how the downhaul works together with the outhaul, vang, and mainsheet.

What Is the Downhaul?

The downhaul is a control used to adjust tension along the luff of the mainsail.

On a dinghy, the downhaul normally pulls the lower end of the sail downward along the mast.

By changing this tension, the sailor can influence the shape of the forward portion of the mainsail.

The Luff of the Sail

The luff is the forward edge of the mainsail, nearest the mast.

The tension along the luff affects where the deepest part of the sail's curvature is located.

This makes the downhaul an important control for adjusting sail shape as wind conditions change.

Tightening the Downhaul

Pulling the downhaul tighter increases tension along the luff of the mainsail.

This generally moves the draft of the sail forward and can flatten the upper and forward sections of the sail.

In stronger wind, additional downhaul tension can therefore help control excessive power.

Stronger Wind

More downhaul tension can help flatten and control the mainsail, making it easier to manage increased wind pressure.

Easing the Downhaul

Easing the downhaul reduces tension along the luff.

This allows the sail to develop more curvature, depending on the design of the sail and the other sail controls.

In lighter wind, the sailor may use less downhaul tension when additional sail power is desirable.

Downhaul and Cunningham

On some dinghies, the downhaul is connected directly to the sail. On others, a separate Cunningham performs a similar function.

Both systems are used to control tension near the lower part of the luff and influence sail shape.

The exact arrangement depends on the boat and sail design.

Downhaul and Wind Conditions

Sail controls should be adjusted according to the conditions rather than left at one setting all day.

As wind strength increases, the sailor may increase downhaul tension to help control the sail.

In lighter wind, excessive downhaul tension may flatten the sail more than necessary.

The sailor should therefore learn to recognize the shape of the sail and make adjustments based on what the boat is telling them.

The Downhaul Works With Other Controls

The downhaul is only one part of the sail-control system.

The mainsheet controls the boom angle.

The outhaul controls tension along the foot and influences the lower sail shape.

The vang controls boom height and helps control leech tension and twist.

The downhaul controls luff tension and influences the forward portion of the sail.

Together, these controls allow the sailor to adjust the mainsail to changing conditions.

Learn to Read the Sail

The purpose of learning sail controls is not to memorize a collection of settings.

The better skill is learning to look at the sail and recognize what needs to change.

An intermediate sailor begins to ask questions such as:

  • Is the sail too full?
  • Is the boat producing too much heel?
  • Is the draft in an efficient position?
  • Has the wind increased?
  • Which sail control should be adjusted?

Practice Exercise

In moderate wind, observe the shape of the mainsail while making small downhaul adjustments.

  1. Begin with a moderate downhaul setting.
  2. Tighten the downhaul gradually.
  3. Observe the forward portion of the sail.
  4. Ease the downhaul gradually.
  5. Compare the change in sail shape.
  6. Try to identify the setting that produces an efficient sail shape for the current wind.

The objective is to develop the ability to read the sail and make purposeful adjustments.

Key Takeaways

  • The downhaul controls luff tension.
  • Downhaul tension influences the shape of the forward portion of the mainsail.
  • More tension can help control sail power in stronger wind.
  • Less tension can allow more sail curvature when additional power is useful.
  • The downhaul works together with the mainsheet, outhaul, and vang.
  • An intermediate sailor learns to read the sail and adjust the controls according to the conditions.
