Module 8

Daggerboard

Learn how the daggerboard helps the boat resist sideways movement and sail effectively upwind.

Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain the purpose of the daggerboard.
  • Explain what is meant by leeway.
  • Understand how the daggerboard provides lateral resistance.
  • Recognize why daggerboard position can change with the point of sail.
  • Understand how the daggerboard works together with the sail and rudder.

What Is a Daggerboard?

A daggerboard is a vertical or nearly vertical foil that extends below the hull of a dinghy.

Its primary purpose is to resist the sideways force generated by the sail.

Without sufficient lateral resistance, much of the force generated by the sail would move the boat sideways instead of helping it travel effectively toward its intended destination.

What Is Leeway?

Leeway is the sideways movement of the boat caused by forces from the wind.

A sailboat does not always travel exactly in the direction its bow is pointing.

Some sideways movement is unavoidable, but the daggerboard helps reduce it.

Side Force From the Sail

When sailing, the wind creates forces on the sail. Some of that force acts in the direction we want the boat to travel, while another component acts sideways.

The daggerboard interacts with the water and produces lateral resistance that helps oppose this sideways component.

This allows the boat to convert more of the sail's aerodynamic force into useful forward movement.

The Daggerboard Upwind

The daggerboard is particularly important when sailing upwind.

When sailing close hauled, the sail produces significant sideways force.

Keeping the daggerboard properly positioned helps the boat resist leeway and maintain its course.

A fully lowered daggerboard generally provides the greatest lateral resistance.

The Daggerboard on a Reach

As the boat bears away from the wind, the sideways force produced by the sail generally changes.

The sailor may be able to raise the daggerboard partially while reaching, depending on the boat and conditions.

The objective is to use enough board to provide the necessary lateral resistance without creating unnecessary drag.

The Daggerboard Downwind

When sailing downwind, the need for lateral resistance is generally reduced.

The daggerboard can therefore often be raised partially or completely, depending on the boat and conditions.

Raising the board reduces the amount of underwater surface area and can reduce drag.

The sailor should always maintain enough board to keep the boat stable and under control.

Daggerboard and Rudder

The daggerboard and rudder both operate in the water, but they have different primary functions.

Daggerboard

Primarily provides lateral resistance and helps reduce leeway.

Rudder

Primarily controls the direction of the boat.

The Daggerboard and Sail Work Together

The daggerboard cannot be considered separately from the sail.

The sail produces aerodynamic forces above the water. The daggerboard provides hydrodynamic resistance below the water.

Together, they allow the sailor to use the wind to move the boat in a controlled direction.

This is an important concept in intermediate sailing: the forces above the water and below the water must work together.

More Daggerboard Is Not Always Better

It may seem that keeping the daggerboard completely down at all times would provide the best performance.

However, an underwater foil also creates drag.

When the boat does not require as much lateral resistance, raising the board can reduce unnecessary drag.

The intermediate sailor learns to use the amount of daggerboard appropriate for the current point of sail and conditions.

Practice Exercise

In safe and moderate conditions, observe how the boat behaves with different daggerboard positions.

  1. Begin sailing upwind with the daggerboard fully lowered.
  2. Observe the boat's ability to maintain its course.
  3. Bear away onto a reach.
  4. Raise the daggerboard slightly and observe the boat's response.
  5. Compare the boat's handling with different board positions.
  6. Return the daggerboard to an appropriate position for the point of sail.

The objective is to develop an understanding of how daggerboard position affects leeway, control, and drag.

Key Takeaways

  • The daggerboard provides lateral resistance.
  • Lateral resistance helps reduce leeway.
  • The daggerboard is particularly important when sailing upwind.
  • Less daggerboard may be needed as the boat bears away from the wind.
  • Raising the board can reduce unnecessary underwater drag.
  • The daggerboard and rudder have different primary functions.
  • The sailor should use enough daggerboard to provide the required lateral resistance without creating unnecessary drag.
