Module 9

Wind Conditions

Learn how changes in wind strength and direction affect the boat and how an intermediate sailor responds to those changes.

Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Recognize changes in wind strength.
  • Recognize changes in wind direction.
  • Explain the difference between a gust and a lull.
  • Adjust sail controls and sailor position as wind conditions change.
  • Anticipate changes rather than simply reacting to them.
  • Select an appropriate sailing response for changing conditions.

Wind Is Not Constant

Wind rarely remains exactly the same while sailing. Its strength and direction can change from moment to moment.

An intermediate sailor learns to observe these changes and make adjustments before the boat becomes difficult to control.

Good sailing is therefore not simply about finding the correct sail setting once. It is about continuously responding to the conditions.

Wind Strength

Wind strength has a major effect on how much force the sails generate.

As wind increases, the sail can produce more force. The boat may accelerate, heel more strongly, and require additional control.

When wind decreases, the boat may lose power and require different sail settings to maintain speed.

Gusts

A gust is a temporary increase in wind strength.

A gust can cause the boat to accelerate and heel more strongly.

An experienced sailor tries to see or feel an approaching gust and prepares for it.

Depending on the conditions, the sailor may ease the mainsheet, move farther to windward, or make other adjustments to maintain control.

Lulls

A lull is a temporary decrease in wind strength.

During a lull, the boat may slow down and the sails may lose power.

The sailor may respond by allowing the sails to develop more power and by avoiding unnecessary movements that disturb the boat.

In light conditions, maintaining momentum becomes especially important.

Changes in Wind Direction

Wind does not always blow from exactly the same direction.

A gradual or sudden change in wind direction can change the boat's point of sail.

The sailor may need to change course or adjust the sails to keep them working efficiently.

Learning to recognize these changes early gives the sailor more time to respond.

Reading the Water

The surface of the water can provide useful clues about wind conditions.

Darker patches, ripples, and changes in the texture of the water can indicate areas of stronger or different wind.

By looking ahead instead of only at the sail, the sailor can sometimes anticipate what the boat will experience next.

Watch the Sail and Tell-Tales

The sail itself also provides information about the wind.

If tell-tales are fitted to the sail, their behavior can help the sailor determine whether the sail is trimmed correctly.

Changes in sail shape, luffing, and leech behavior can also provide clues about the wind and the effectiveness of the current sail trim.

Adjusting to the Wind

An intermediate sailor has several tools available when conditions change.

  • Mainsheet: adjust boom angle and sail power.
  • Outhaul: adjust the shape of the lower sail.
  • Vang: control boom height, leech tension, and twist.
  • Downhaul: adjust luff tension and sail shape.
  • Sailor position: control balance and heel.
  • Daggerboard: manage lateral resistance and leeway.

The important skill is knowing which adjustment is appropriate for the situation.

Anticipate — Don't Just React

One of the biggest differences between a beginner and an intermediate sailor is anticipation.

A beginner may wait until the boat heels heavily before reacting to a gust.

An intermediate sailor looks ahead, feels the changing wind, and prepares before the gust arrives.

The same principle applies to lulls, shifts, waves, and changes in boat traffic.

Know When Conditions Are Too Strong

Good seamanship includes recognizing when conditions exceed the sailor's ability, the boat's capability, or the available safety margin.

More skill does not mean that every condition should be sailed.

An intermediate sailor should be willing to reduce sail, return to shore, or postpone sailing when conditions become unsafe.

Practice Exercise

During a sailing session, spend several minutes observing the wind before making major changes to your controls.

  1. Look at the water ahead of the boat.
  2. Identify areas where the wind appears stronger or weaker.
  3. Watch the sails for changes in pressure.
  4. Feel changes in wind strength on your face.
  5. Observe how the boat responds to each change.
  6. Make small control adjustments before the boat becomes difficult to handle.

The objective is to develop the habit of looking ahead, anticipating changes, and responding smoothly.

Key Takeaways

  • Wind strength and direction are constantly changing.
  • A gust is an increase in wind strength.
  • A lull is a decrease in wind strength.
  • Changes in wind direction can change the boat's point of sail.
  • The water, sails, and tell-tales can provide clues about changing wind conditions.
  • Sail controls, sailor position, and daggerboard position should be adjusted as conditions change.
  • An intermediate sailor learns to anticipate rather than simply react.
  • Good seamanship includes knowing when conditions are beyond the safe limits of the sailor or boat.
