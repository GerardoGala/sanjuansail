Module 4

Outhaul

Learn how the outhaul controls the shape of the lower part of the mainsail and influences sail power.

Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Identify the purpose of the outhaul.
  • Explain how outhaul tension changes the shape of the mainsail.
  • Recognize the difference between a fuller and flatter sail.
  • Understand why outhaul tension should change with wind conditions.
  • Use the outhaul as part of an overall sail-trim system.

What Is the Outhaul?

The outhaul is a control line that adjusts the tension along the foot of the mainsail.

It normally runs from the clew of the mainsail toward the aft end of the boom.

By tightening or easing the outhaul, the sailor can change the shape of the lower portion of the sail.

Sail Shape

A sail does not have to remain the same shape in every wind condition.

A fuller sail generally produces more power. A flatter sail generally reduces power and can be useful when the wind is stronger.

The outhaul is one of the controls the sailor uses to make this adjustment.

Tightening the Outhaul

Pulling the outhaul tighter increases tension along the foot of the sail.

This generally makes the lower portion of the mainsail flatter.

A flatter sail can reduce power and help control the boat when the wind is strong.

Stronger Wind

More outhaul tension can help flatten the sail, reducing excess power and helping the sailor maintain control.

Easing the Outhaul

Easing the outhaul reduces tension along the foot of the mainsail.

This allows the lower portion of the sail to become fuller.

A fuller sail can generate more power and can therefore be useful when the wind is light.

Lighter Wind

Less outhaul tension can allow a fuller sail, helping the boat generate useful power in lighter conditions.

Outhaul and Wind Conditions

There is no single outhaul setting that is correct for every situation.

In lighter wind, the sailor may ease the outhaul to create a fuller sail.

As the wind increases, the sailor may tighten the outhaul to flatten the sail and reduce excess power.

The objective is not simply to make the sail as flat as possible. The objective is to use the sail shape that best matches the current conditions.

Outhaul and Boat Balance

Sail shape affects the amount and distribution of aerodynamic force produced by the sail.

When the sail produces more power, the boat may heel more strongly.

The sailor can therefore use the outhaul together with body position, boom angle, and other sail controls to maintain a balanced and efficient boat.

This is an important idea in intermediate sailing: the controls work together.

Outhaul vs. Mainsheet

The mainsheet and outhaul both affect the mainsail, but they perform different jobs.

Mainsheet

Primarily controls the position of the boom and the sail's angle relative to the wind.

Outhaul

Primarily controls the tension along the foot of the sail and therefore influences the lower sail shape.

Outhaul and Point of Sail

The best outhaul setting depends on both the wind conditions and the point of sail. Use these settings as practical starting points, then adjust based on boat speed and control.

Think of the sail shape as a belly. A Full outhaul setting allows the mainsail to have a bigger belly, creating a fuller sail shape. A Flat outhaul setting pulls the foot tighter, making the mainsail have a flatter belly.

Try It Yourself

Use the simulator to experiment with the outhaul. Try Flat, Base, and Full settings and observe how they affect the boat.


Practice Exercise

In light to moderate wind, observe the lower part of the mainsail while making small adjustments to the outhaul.

  1. Begin with the outhaul at a normal setting.
  2. Ease the outhaul slightly and observe the change in the lower part of the sail.
  3. Tighten the outhaul gradually and observe how the sail becomes flatter.
  4. Notice how the boat responds to the change in sail power.
  5. Return the outhaul to an efficient setting for the current wind.

The objective is to develop a visual and practical understanding of how outhaul tension changes sail shape.

Key Takeaways

  • The outhaul controls tension along the foot of the mainsail.
  • Tightening the outhaul generally makes the lower sail flatter.
  • Easing the outhaul generally allows the lower sail to become fuller.
  • A fuller sail can provide useful power in lighter wind.
  • A flatter sail can help control excess power in stronger wind.
  • The outhaul works together with the mainsheet and the other sail controls.
